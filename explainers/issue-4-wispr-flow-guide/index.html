If I Could, Would You?Issue #4: Context Management

Get Wispr Flow on your phone

Honestly, you can skip this page. Download the app, do what it tells you, and you're done. It's built so you don't need me.

But if you want someone to hold the flashlight, here's the short version. I'm not affiliated with Wispr. Apps change, so I link their own help page under each step.

iPhone

Three steps

  1. Download it and open it once. The keyboard won't show up in your settings until you do.
  2. Add the keyboard. Settings, General, Keyboard, Keyboards, Add New Keyboard, then pick Wispr Flow. Tap it in the list and switch on Allow Full Access. It needs that to work.
  3. Talk. In any text box, tap the globe key to switch to Flow, press the button and go. Say yes when it asks for the microphone. If you said no once, turn it on in Settings.

Stuck? Their page: Set up the Flow keyboard on iPhone.

Android

Three steps

  1. Check you're on Android 13 or newer, then install it from Google Play and open it.
  2. Say yes to the three things it asks for: the microphone, display over other apps, and the Accessibility service. That last one is how it types into other apps.
  3. Talk. Tap any text box, a little button shows up, press it and go.

Got a work profile on your phone? Install it on the personal side. It can't switch on Accessibility from a work profile. Their pages: install on Android and requirements.

Worth knowing

About those permissions

Full Access and Accessibility sound scary because they're powerful. A keyboard can see what you type, so read what you're agreeing to and decide if you're comfortable with it. Don't talk passwords, card numbers or other people's private details into it. On a work phone, check with your company first.

Then what

Use it on the Vision Quest

Open The Context Vision Quest, tap any answer box and talk. Ramble. Change your mind halfway through. Wispr cleans it up as you go. Or skip the app: the page has its own Talk button that works with nothing installed.
